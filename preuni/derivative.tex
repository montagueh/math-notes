\section{Limits}
To calculate the derivitive, we look at the change over time $h$,
\[
  \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \qquad h \neq 0
\]

So for the function $f(x) = x^{2}$,
\begin{align*}
  f'(x) &= \frac{(x+h)^{2} - x^{2}}{h} \\
        &= \frac{\cancel{x^{2}} + 2xh + h^{2} - \cancel{x^{2}}}{h} \\
        &= \frac{2xh + h^{2}}{h} \\
        &= \frac{\cancel{h}(2x + h)}{\cancel{h}} \\
        &= 2x + h \\
  \lim_{h \to 0} f'(x) &= 2x
\end{align*}

For function $f(x) = \sqrt[2]{x}$,
\begin{align*}
  f'(x) &= \frac{\sqrt[2]{x+h} - \sqrt[2]{x}{h} \\
        &= \frac{\sqrt[2]{x+h} - \sqrt[2]{x}}{h} \cdot \frac{\sqrt[2]{x+h} + \sqrt[2]{x}}{\sqrt[2]{x+h} + \sqrt[2]{x}} \\
        &= \frac{\cancel{x} + h - \cancel{x}}{h(\sqrt[2]{x+h} + \sqrt[2]{x})}
\end{align*}
